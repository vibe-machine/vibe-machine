The semantic web failed.
Semantics didn’t.

For twenty years “semantics” meant committees, standards nobody shipped and diagrams nobody ran. Developers learned to flinch at the word. Fair enough.

But the idea was never wrong. Data should say what it is. The price was wrong. Getting people to agree on what things mean took a thousand meetings, so we stored the values, skipped the meaning, and built an industry to clean up after ourselves.

Agents changed the price. They draft the model, argue the edge cases and write the queries. Agents don’t mind meetings.

So we’re taking the idea back. Not for the web. For the database your software runs on.

Meaning from the start.

An operational ontology is a working database that holds your data together with what each thing is and how things relate, exactly enough that it refuses data that does not fit. Your software and agents read and write it as they work.

The best-known tools that add meaning to data arrive later, as a layer over a warehouse. This is the store itself. The Vibe Machine runs on one too.

Types, read from the live store. Every type says what it is, what it is a kind of, and which questions use it.

In an independent test of 12,751 questions over 95 databases, short notes on what the data means raised correct answers from 35% to 55% for an AI model, and from 72% to 93% for people. BIRD benchmark, NeurIPS 2023.

Ship it as a package.

A finished model ships as a package: its types, what each one means, the questions it answers with their queries, and sample data. Install one and start asking.

Plan the work, design it, and supervise the agents that write the code. When it works, Flex records a demo video of what you built.

You steer.

Agents do the work. You keep them on course, the way you keep a car in its lane: a little left, a little right. That is why the picture on this app is a dial.

The doctrine.

We made the choices, so you can get straight to work: a strongly typed database (TypeDB), one shared foundation of types (gist), and a method that starts from your questions.

1Meaning goes in first. Not into a layer added later.
2Questions before models. Say what it must answer. The model follows.
3A query proves it, or it isn’t done. Every question runs. Every answer is checked.
4The store is the application. Not a report about it.
5Small models beat grand ones. Build what the questions need and stop.
6Progress beats purity. Ship the model. Fix it when a question fails.
7Agents do the work. You steer. A little left, a little right.
8It runs on your Mac, and it’s yours. Change anything.

Install

  1. Download the disk image, VibeMachine.dmg, and open it.
  2. Drag the dial into the ring on the machine. This copies the app to your Applications folder.
  3. In Applications, open One. That is the Vibe Machine; the file’s name has not caught up yet.
